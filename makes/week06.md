## Process Notes
- Short text: Ozymandias( By Percy Bysshe Shelley)
- Using GPT: Speaker and Narrative Structure
+ The poem uses a framed narrative: the speaker recounts a story told by a traveller from an “antique land.”
+ This distancing technique creates layers of narration, suggesting that knowledge of the past is indirect and fragmented.
+ The traveller functions as a witness to history, emphasizing how time separates present observers from past power.
- Irony:
+ The central rhetorical strategy is dramatic irony.
+ Ozymandias proclaims: “Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!” intending to intimidate rivals.
+ However, the surrounding description shows that nothing remains of his empire, turning the boast into a symbol of arrogance and failure.
+ The irony highlights the temporary nature of political power.
- Imagery
+ Shelley uses visual imagery of ruin and desolation:
* “vast and trunkless legs of stone”
* “shattered visage”
* “lone and level sands”
=>These images contrast monumental ambition with physical decay.
The barren desert setting reinforces the emptiness left after the fall of empire.
- Symbolism
+ The broken statue symbolizes the collapse of human power and pride.
+ The desert represents the vast force of time and nature, which ultimately erases human achievements.
+ The inscription on the pedestal symbolizes the ruler’s hubris and desire for immortality.
- Tone:
+ The tone is detached and reflective, rather than emotional.
+ This calm description intensifies the poem’s mockery of Ozymandias’s arrogance.
+ The contrast between the king’s proud words and the quiet ruin creates a subtle but powerful critique of tyranny.
- Role of the Sculptor
+ The poem emphasizes the sculptor’s ability to capture the ruler’s character: “its sculptor well those passions read.”
+ This suggests that art preserves truth longer than political power.
+ The sculptor ironically immortalizes Ozymandias’s arrogance, not his greatness.
- Theme / Message
+ Human power and glory are temporary.
+ Time and nature ultimately destroy even the greatest empires.
+ The poem criticizes hubris, tyranny, and the illusion of permanence in political authority.

## Reflection
When machines “read” literature for us, we gain new ways of seeing patterns in a text, but we may also lose some of the depth and ambiguity that comes from human interpretation. Tools like Voyant allow readers to observe structural features of a text, such as word frequency, recurring vocabulary, and patterns across different sections. In the case of Ozymandias, Voyant highlights how certain words, such as “Ozymandias,” “works,” or “wreck,” appear repeatedly, which helps reveal the poem’s focus on power and decay. This type of analysis can uncover patterns that might not be immediately visible through traditional close reading.
However, machines cannot fully experience the emotional or cultural resonance of literature. While Voyant can show which words appear frequently, it cannot explain why those words are meaningful in a historical or symbolic context. This is where GPT functions differently. GPT attempts to interpret the text by identifying themes, rhetorical strategies, and symbolism. For example, it interprets the broken statue as a symbol of the collapse of human power and reads the irony in the inscription left by Ozymandias.
In this way, Voyant and GPT imagine “meaning” differently. Voyant treats meaning as something that emerges from patterns in language, focusing on measurable textual data. GPT, on the other hand, treats meaning as interpretation, connecting textual elements to broader ideas such as power, time, and human ambition. While both tools provide useful insights, neither fully replaces human reading. Instead, they function best as complementary tools that support, rather than replace, literary interpretation.

## Attribution & AI Use
- Text Used: “Ozymandias” by Percy Bysshe Shelley.
- Source: Public domain poem (commonly available through poetry archives and educational websites).
- Tools Used: 
 + Voyant Tools (https://voyant-tools.org) for computational text analysis.
 + GPT for generating a literary and rhetorical interpretation of the poem.
- GPT Prompt(s): I am analyzing Percy Shelley’s poem Ozymandias for a digital humanities assignment. Please provide a rhetorical and literary analysis of the poem. Focus on how irony, imagery, and symbolism contribute to the theme of the impermanence of power. Also explain how the narrative structure (the traveller telling the story) shapes the reader’s understanding of history and authority. I used this prompt to guide GPT toward analyzing specific rhetorical elements in the poem rather than producing a general summary. I focused on irony, imagery, and narrative structure because these elements are central to the poem’s critique of political power.
- What Each Tool Could Not Do: 
 + Voyant Tools can identify patterns such as word frequency, trends, and collocations, but it cannot explain the meaning of those patterns. For example, while it shows which words appear frequently, it does not interpret symbolism, tone, or historical context.
+ GPT can generate interpretive explanations of themes, imagery, and rhetorical strategies, but it does not provide quantitative analysis of the text. It also relies on general knowledge rather than directly visualizing linguistic patterns within the specific dataset.
