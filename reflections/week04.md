# Week 4 Reflection

Reflect 4: Is AI a collaborator, tool, or plagiarist in storytelling?

## Key Insight
The answer depends on *transparency and intention*. AI is a collaborator when I acknowledge its role, credit its contribution, and maintain narrative authority over the final work. It's a tool—like grammar check or thesaurus—when I use it for specific, bounded tasks without pretending they required human creativity. It becomes plagiarism when I present AI-generated content as wholly my own without acknowledgment, especially when the training data included others' work. The gray zone is vast: most storytelling with AI falls somewhere between tool and collaboration. When ChatGPT helps me brainstorm plot holes, that's tool use. When I feed it a concept and iterate on its suggestions, we're collaborating—but unevenly, since I retain choice and the AI doesn't. The plagiarism risk is real: AI models trained on copyrighted works can reproduce those stories. The ethical path forward isn't rejecting AI but making its role *legible*. Tell readers where AI assisted. Credit the training data's origins. Make choices visible. Storytelling with AI isn't inherently dishonest; invisible storytelling with AI is.

## Example
If I use ChatGPT to generate dialogue and credit it ("This dialogue was co-written with AI"), that's honest collaboration. If I pass it off as my own creation without disclosure, I'm both plagiarizing training data and deceiving readers.

## What I'm Learning
- AI assistance exists on a spectrum—not binary categories
- Honesty about process matters more than whether AI was used
- Plagiarism is about attribution, not the tool itself

## Next Steps
