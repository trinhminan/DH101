# Week 3 Reflection

Reflect 3: What does “authenticity” mean when identity is co-constructed with AI?
## Key Insight
Authenticity was never fully autonomous—it was always relational. We construct identity through mirrors: family, culture, media, peer response. AI doesn't destroy authenticity; it makes its constructed nature visible. When I edit my face with AI filters, align my language with algorithmic recommendations, or shape my online presence through AI-curated content, I'm not losing authenticity—I'm experiencing its *scaffolding*. The authentic "me" isn't hidden beneath these layers; it's partially constituted through them. My choices about how to present myself, what filters to use, which AI tool to trust—those choices are where authenticity lives now. The risk isn't inauthenticity per se; it's *invisible influence*. If I don't know which of my preferences are mine versus algorithmically suggested, I lose agency. Authenticity in the age of AI means being aware of the co-construction—acknowledging that I'm not a sovereign self, but a self continuously negotiated with technology, data, and other people's choices embedded in code.

## Example
When I edit a photo with AI upscaling or beauty filters, is the result less "me"? The unfiltered version also isn't neutral—it's shaped by camera angle, lighting, my choice of what to photograph. Both versions are constructed. The authentic choice is the conscious one: I know why I'm editing, what I'm choosing to show or hide.

## What I'm Learning
- Authenticity is about agency and awareness, not purity
- All identity construction is mediated—AI just makes this visible
- The question isn't whether AI influences me, but whether I'm conscious of that influence
