# Week 8 Reflection

Reflect 8: Who is visible/invisible in AI networks? How does visualization reveal or obscure power?

## Key Insight
Visualization is never neutral—it's argument. When we map a network, our choice of what to show, how to position nodes, which connections to emphasize *shapes* what we conclude about power. AI networks appear democratic: everyone connected, relationships rendered equal. But network visualization can hide hierarchy. A node might seem "central" because of *visible* connections while invisible labor—content moderation, data annotation, maintenance—goes unseen. The most powerful people in networks are often the ones off-screen: the engineers who designed the visualization, the companies controlling the data, the governments accessing the network. Visualization democratizes in one sense—makes relationships visible—but obscures in another. A social network graph shows who communicates but erases *context*: Why do these connections exist? Who benefited from building these networks? Whose data was extracted? Who is *not* in the network because they lack access? AI networks, especially, contain invisible actors: the training data, the corporations licensing models, the data brokers. These are visible only if we *choose* to visualize them. The question isn't whether visualization reveals truth, but whose perspective it embodies. Powerful visualization reveals power by making visible what was hidden—the labor, the data extraction, the asymmetries. Obscuring visualization hides these same things behind seemingly neutral graphs.

## Example
A network graph of social media might show millions of users as equal nodes. But the real power lies with the platform, the algorithm, the advertisers—the infrastructure you can't see in the visualization.

## What I'm Learning
- Visualization is choice, not truth
- The most powerful actors in networks are often invisible
- Ethical visualization means making power structures visible, not hidden
