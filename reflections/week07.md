# Week 7 Reflection

Reflect 7: How does AI reshape global geographies of power, labor, and environment?

## Key Insight
AI doesn't erase geography—it *reorganizes* it. The fantasy is that AI is placeless, floating in the cloud. Reality: every AI model depends on physical infrastructure—data centers consuming massive electricity, rare earth minerals mined from specific territories, human labor concentrated in low-wage regions. Global power dynamics don't disappear; they hide. Control over AI becomes control over digital infrastructure, which concentrates in wealthy nations and corporations. Labor becomes increasingly invisible: data labelers in Kenya, content moderators in Manila, all making decisions that train the models used globally. AI reproduces colonial patterns—exporting labor from Global South, exporting environmental costs. A model trained to recognize faces is trained *by* whom? Where was that data collected? Who profits from its accuracy? These questions have geography. The promise of AI erasing location is the promise of elite geography—the perspective of someone who controls infrastructure and can outsource costs. Environmental impact is similarly concentrated: data centers draw enormous power, often from coal and natural gas. AI doesn't transcend geography; it *depends* on it while obscuring that dependence. Reckoning with AI means mapping these material connections, making visible where AI is actually made and at what cost.

## Example
A AI model trained on facial recognition data might have been labeled by workers in countries with weaker privacy protections, using electricity from coal plants, sold to governments that surveilled their own citizens.

## What I'm Learning
- AI is deeply material and geographic, despite feeling abstract
- Global inequities are embedded in AI infrastructure
- Environmental cost of AI is a hidden externality we rarely see
