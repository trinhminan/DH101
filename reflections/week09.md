# Week 9 Reflection

Reflect 9: What is creativity when AI can generate endlessly? Where is the human in generative work?

## Key Insight
Creativity becomes *curation*. When machines can produce infinite variations, the human role shifts from generation to *selection and intention*. The original creative act—having an idea no one else had—remains distinctly human. But once that idea exists, machines can generate a million variations. This isn't loss; it's transformation. Where is the human in generative work? In the *prompt*, the judgment call, the decision to publish or discard, the choice to push the model in unexpected directions. A generative AI doesn't have taste; it has statistics. The human brings *why*—why does this matter? Which of the thousand generated images actually speaks to the vision? What story am I telling through selection? Creativity shifts upstream: the creative person is the one who knows what to ask the machine for, who recognizes when a generated output is *interesting* rather than just *competent*, who understands the gap between what the model produced and what's *actually meaningful*. This is still creative labor—it's curatorial intelligence. The risk is that curation becomes invisible; people think creativity has left the picture. It hasn't—it's just become less visible, less dramatic. The person who generates 1000 versions isn't more creative than the person who recognizes one as magnificent.

## Example
An artist using image generation isn't less creative than a painter. Both are making choices. The painter chooses brushstrokes; the artist chooses prompts and selections. Both are expressing intention through decision.

## What I'm Learning
- Creativity persists but transforms with AI
- Selection and judgment are creative acts
- Invisible labor in curation is still labor
