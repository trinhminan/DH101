# Week 12 Reflection

Reflect 12: Is AI sustainable? What ecological trade-offs are we willing to accept?

## Key Insight
AI is not sustainable—it's extractive. Every model requires data collection, computation, infrastructure. Training a large language model consumes gigawatt-hours of electricity, equivalent to the annual power consumption of dozens of households. The data required often comes from harvesting global information without compensation to its creators. Then the model's predictions shape decisions for billions, concentrating power. This is a *unidirectional transfer*: extraction from the many for concentration in the few. The sustainability question is political, not technical. We could build smaller, slower, more local AI systems. Instead, we're building larger ones because scale drives profit. The ecological cost is paid by people who don't benefit: communities near data centers breathing polluted air, countries in climate zones already ravaged by extreme weather bearing the energy burden, workers in resource extraction enduring hazardous conditions mining lithium and cobalt. Sustainability requires *limits*—caps on model size, requirements for renewable energy, mandates to minimize training, pressure on corporations to account for their full environmental cost. But the industry incentivizes the opposite: bigger, faster, more powerful. The real question isn't technical sustainability but political will. Can we choose smaller AI? Can we legislate environmental costs into prices? Or will we accept an extractive status quo because the benefits accrue to wealthy nations and corporations?

## Example
Training a single large model produces as much carbon as a long-distance flight. We do this thousands of times annually. The environmental cost is massive and largely invisible to users.

## What I'm Learning
- AI's environmental cost is a hidden externality
- "Sustainable AI" might be an oxymoron without fundamental changes
- The choice is political—do we want to limit AI for ecological reasons?
