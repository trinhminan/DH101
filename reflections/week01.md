# Week 1 Reflection

**Reflect 1:** What counts as "making" in the age of AI? What might be gained/lost when machines participate in making?

## Key Insight
Making is no longer about the hand alone—it's about intention, judgment, and interpretation. When I engage with AI tools like generative networks, I'm not making in the traditional sense, but I am *making meaning* by choosing what to create, how to frame it, and what questions to ask about it. The tool extends my creative possibility, but it also obscures the labor and data behind the scenes.

## Example: This Person Does Not Exist
The faces on This Person Does Not Exist are entirely machine-generated, yet they provoke deeply human responses. When I viewed dozens of synthetic faces, I became aware of patterns: the slight blur in the background, the subtle perfection of symmetry, the demographic distribution. This Person Does Not Exist didn't make a conscious choice—but I did, by selecting it as an object to study and writing about what I noticed.

That's where making happens: in the *selection, reflection, and articulation*, not necessarily in the raw generation.

## What I'm Learning
- Making with AI requires new language. "Authorship" becomes fragmented across data labelers, engineers, and users.
- Machines don't have intent, but they do have patterns baked into their training. Using AI is never neutral—it's using someone else's aesthetic choices.
- The boundary between making and consuming is blurry. Curation is a form of making.

## Next Steps
Next week, I want to explore what authenticity means when AI can generate endlessly. I'm also curious about consent: when my image could be used to train the next model, what rights do I have? What do I owe to the people whose faces train these systems?
