# Sustainability & Ethics

## My Research Summary

My research into the environmental impact of artificial intelligence reveals that the “cloud” is far from abstract; it is a physical, resource-heavy infrastructure. AI’s cost is a planetary one, beginning with extractive mining for lithium and rare earth minerals required for hardware. Once active, these systems have an immense energy hunger. For example, training a single large model like GPT-3 has been reported as emitting as much CO₂ as a car driving 1.2 million miles. Beyond carbon, server cooling consumes significant amounts of water—roughly half a liter for every 20 to 50 queries. Overall, the industry is currently on a path of compute maximalism, where speed and accuracy often override ecological limits.

## Personal Reflection

### My AI Use Habits
Over one week, I conducted 64 ChatGPT queries. My use was uneven: it peaked on Sunday (18 queries) and Wednesday (17 queries), and dropped during the rest of the week.

### Estimated Impact
At 0.15g CO₂ per query, my weekly footprint was approximately **9.6g of CO₂**. My usage also indirectly consumed around **1 to 1.5 liters of water** for data center cooling.

### What I’m Willing to Change
I am willing to adopt a politics of refusal for trivial tasks. I will use concise prompts to reduce processing time and choose smaller, more efficient models for basic information instead of energy-heavy systems.

### What I Can’t/Won’t Change
I cannot fully stop using AI for complex academic research or coding. As several sources note, researchers often depend on large-scale computing to stay competitive, and these tools are now embedded in global workflows that are difficult to fully opt out of without professional penalties.

## Systemic vs. Individual

### What Individuals Can Do
Users can practice green prompting by avoiding unnecessary follow-up queries and choosing services that prioritize transparency and environmental reporting. We can also demand stronger ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) standards from the platforms we use.

### What Requires Systemic Change
True sustainability requires a broader transition to renewable-powered data centers and more energy-efficient hardware. It also requires moving away from a “bigger is better” mindset by adopting methods like model pruning and knowledge distillation to reduce inference costs.

### Who Is Responsible?
Users have some responsibility, but the primary responsibility lies with the major AI companies that control planetary-scale computation, including OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google. Governments are also responsible for setting and enforcing power limits, transparency requirements, and regulations that make extraction and energy costs visible to the public.
